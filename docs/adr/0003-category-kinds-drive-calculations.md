# Calculations read subcategory kinds, never category names

Fixed cost, savings rate and the reserve target all depend on telling income, fixed spending, variable spending and internal transfers apart, and households will rename, add and reassign subcategories over time. We therefore give every subcategory exactly one kind, `income | fixed | variable | transfer`, and every calculation in `packages/core` reads only the kind. Category and subcategory names exist for people; no rule, sum or ranking ever branches on a name.

## Decision

- Categories are fixed by the product; households cannot create them. Subcategories are shipped by the product with a default kind, and a household may add its own subcategories and override the kind of any subcategory for itself.
- Credit-card purchases are spending on the purchase date, each installment on its own date; the card bill payment is an internal transfer, never spending.
- Internal transfers are detected by pair matching: same amount, opposite direction, at most two business days apart, both accounts assigned to the household, confirmed when the counterpart document hash stored on the transaction matches the keyed hash of one of the household's synced account holders (see ADR-0008; the document itself is never stored). Pairing belongs to the `ledger` module and is re-run whenever an account joins the household. A user can always mark a transaction as an internal transfer by hand.
- Precedence: manual categorization beats household rules, household rules beat product defaults, product defaults beat the provider's own category. Recurring-spend detection only suggests a `fixed` kind; nothing changes kind silently.
- Fixed cost is the sum of spending in subcategories of kind `fixed`. Average fixed cost is the mean over the last six complete months, computed with at least three; with fewer, the value is labelled an estimate that names the months used. Reserve target = reserve multiple (default 6, range 3 to 12) × average fixed cost, recomputed at month close; the household is notified only when it moves by more than 10%.

## Consequences

- Unit tests for every calculation use fixtures keyed by kind, so renaming a subcategory can never break a test or a number.
- Any new kind is a domain change that touches every calculation; it needs a new ADR, not a migration.
- A transaction with no subcategory has no kind and is reported as uncategorized; it counts in no total until categorized. This makes gaps visible instead of silently miscounting them.

## Amendment 2026-09-26 (#15): only manual choices are stored

The taxonomy ships as data in `packages/core/src/ledger/categories/` (thirteen fixed categories, each subcategory with its default kind), together with the mapping from Pluggy's category names and a short list of product default rules for unambiguous statement descriptions (card bill payment, condominium, IPTU, IOF, and similar). A provider category that is too generic to guess (a plain PIX or TED transfer, "Other", a bare top-level bucket) maps to nothing, so the transaction is reported as uncategorized instead of landing in a catch-all.

Categorization is resolved by one pure function each time transactions are read, in the precedence above; only a member's manual choice is persisted (`transaction_categorization`, keyed by transaction and scoped through its account like the transaction itself). Household rules, subcategories and kind overrides are household-scoped tables. Consequences: a rule applies at once to every past and future transaction, so "apply on sync" and "apply on demand" are the same thing; an account moved to another household keeps its manual choices, as ADR-0001 requires, while the destination's rules replace the source's (a manual choice pointing at a subcategory only the source household has falls back to the destination's rules); and nothing needs re-running after a sync, a rule change or a move. A rule matches when its normalized pattern appears as a whole-word sequence in the normalized description (upper case, accents and punctuation stripped); when several rules match, the most specific (more words, then longer) wins, then the newest. Recurring-spend detection runs over the last three complete months and only lists subcategories a member may mark as fixed.

## Amendment 2026-09-28 (#16): internal transfers are paired at read time

Pairing runs where categorization runs: each time transactions are read, one pure function in `packages/core/src/ledger/transfers/` pairs the household's transactions and resolves each one's categorization and kind. Only a member's internal-transfer mark is persisted (`internal_transfer_mark`, keyed by transaction and scoped through its account, so it travels with a moved account). "Re-run when an account joins the household" therefore holds by construction: the next read pairs over the accounts the household has now, and a pair whose other account left is simply not found again.

- A pair is a debit and a credit on two different accounts of the household, same currency and same absolute amount (direction comes from the transaction type, never from the sign, because Pluggy signs card amounts the other way round), at most two business days apart. Business days are weekdays; national holidays are not modelled, and a miss is what the manual mark is for. The counterpart document on each leg is evidence, read against the other leg's account holder: the same document confirms; a different CPF rejects (the money went to or came from another person) unless that CPF is itself one of the household's account holders, which covers a joint account reported under the other partner's document; a different CNPJ says nothing, because a card bill paid by Pix or boleto names the card issuer and a move to a broker names the broker. When the other leg's holder is unknown (a bank that does not send it), a document that belongs to any of the household's account holders confirms and anything else says nothing, so a missing holder never hides a real transfer. A candidate with a confirming leg and no rejecting one is confirmed; one with no evidence either way still pairs, unconfirmed.
- When one transaction could pair with several, candidates are ranked (confirmed first, then fewer business days, then fewer calendar days, then transaction ids) and taken greedily, so the result does not depend on read order and no transaction is in two pairs.
- Precedence, highest first: internal-transfer mark, manual categorization, detected pair, household rule, product default, provider category. A mark of "not a transfer" also drops a transfer-kind categorization, leaving the transaction uncategorized for the member to choose. A manual categorization of a non-transfer kind keeps the transaction out of pairing.
- An internal transfer's kind is always `transfer`, whatever kind override the household set; its subcategory is the one it already resolved to when that is of kind transfer (an investment movement, a card bill caught by the default rule), else "Pagamento de fatura" when either account is a credit card, else "Entre contas da casa".
- A month is read with a margin on each side of twice the longest calendar span two business days can cover (eight days, `pairingReadRange`), so a transaction near the month boundary is read together with its own candidates and with the candidates that compete for them, and pairs the same way in both months. A longer chain of equal amounts days apart can still pair differently in the two months; the manual mark settles such a case.
- Credit cards: Pluggy lists each installment as its own transaction, dated on its own day with the installment's amount (the original purchase date and total live in `creditCardMetadata`, which Feudo does not read), so storing the transaction date is what "each installment on its own date" means. The bill payment is a checking debit paired with the card's credit; when the card is not connected, the product default rule for "PAGAMENTO FATURA" already gives it kind transfer.
- Income is the sum of transactions of kind income (credits add, debits subtract) and spending the sum of kinds fixed and variable (debits add, credits such as refunds subtract), per currency. Internal transfers and uncategorized transactions count in neither.

## Amendment 2026-10-01 (#20): the reserve target is recorded once a day and notifies above a 10% move

The reserve target (reserve multiple × average fixed cost) needs two different things from the same calculation: a page that always shows the live number, and a notice that only fires on a real move, not on every daily recomputation's small wobble. We keep `packages/core` the single source for both and split "live" from "recorded" at the web layer.

- `packages/core/src/ledger/dashboard.ts` exports `averageFixedCost({ month, lines })`, the average fixed cost calculation made public, alongside a per-window-month breakdown (`fixedCentavos: number | null`, `null` a gap). `buildLedgerDashboard` is rewritten to call it, so the Visão geral tile and the Reserva page read the exact same number from the exact same function; neither can drift from the other by having its own copy of the window logic.
- `packages/core/src/reserve/` adds `computeReserveTarget(average, multiple)` (the reserve multiple's `3..12` range constants move here from `households/validation.ts`, the single source now; the Reserva page and onboarding form import them from `@feudo/core`) and `shouldNotifyReserveTargetChange`, comparing a newly computed target against the previous one **rescaled to the current multiple** (`previousAverage × currentMultiple`) before asking whether it moved by more than 10% (`RESERVE_TARGET_NOTICE_THRESHOLD_BASIS_POINTS = 1000`, integer basis-point math, strictly greater than, not at or above). Rescaling first is what makes a multiple change alone unable to trigger a notice: the household's own average fixed cost did not move, so the rescaled comparison never does either.
- The web layer (`apps/web/src/modules/reserve/`) adds two household-scoped tables: `reserve_target_record` (one row per household per closed month: the average, months used, estimate flag, multiple, target and currency used) and `reserve_target_notice` (created only when the rule above fires: the rescaled previous target, the new target, and a per-household `dismissed_at`). The daily job (`runReserveMonthCloseStep`, composed into `/api/cron/daily`) closes the household-local month before the current one (resolved in the household's own time zone, default America/Sao_Paulo) for every household, skipping a household already closed for that month (idempotent) or with no categorized month in its window at all. The Reserva page itself never reads these tables for its own numbers: it computes the same live window the job would use right now, through the same `readHouseholdDashboardLines` entry point the ledger slice exports (shared with the overview), so a member never sees the page disagree with what the next month close will record. The record and notice tables exist only to back that one comparison — "did the target move enough since last time to say something" — and the dismissible notice itself; a month-close history view is not built yet (future work).

### Consequences

- A household that only changes its reserve multiple sees the Reserva page's live numbers update immediately, with no record written and no notice created, even though the multiple is itself a household-settings write.
- The month-close job is append-only and per-household error-isolated (one household's failure, caught, counted and logged, never fails the run for the rest), matching the shape `sync.syncAllConnections` already established for the same reason.
